Scope and constraints

  • The initial runtime audit was followed by Rust runtime, standard-library, compiler, and language-service improvements. All three sequential measurement tasks are complete and retained: Substring, project-aware LSP composition, and warm project builds. Each passed format, build, complete workspace tests, and repeated benchmark comparisons.
  • Retained changes have recorded build/test and benchmark evidence. Later focused workloads have narrower timing scope than the original full-suite interpreter comparisons; those limits are listed explicitly.
  • Absolute benchmark times are machine-specific; the reported improvements use same-checkout before/after comparisons.
  • FPAS syntax and semantics are held fixed. Rust internals, bytecode, runtime values, compiler/runtime seams, and artifact formats may be replaced.
  • New speed claims remain hypotheses until they pass the project workflow: save baseline, change one variable, release build, compare the full relevant suite, then record only a settled win.

Recommended reading

Start with the retained follow-up results and completed follow-ups and remaining gates. The original interpreter stages and all five allocation follow-ups are closed as accepted or rejected. That closes those experiments, not all performance work. The Substring comparison retained a roughly 99% ASCII and 79% Unicode gain in its focused workloads. Project-aware LSP composition now also has a measured improvement: roughly 89% for warm and overlapping requests and 22% for edits. The startup follow-up now retains a 23–25% warm-build improvement from avoiding redundant path canonicalization. The complete TUI test group passed; headless TUI and Notes timings remained stable. Further candidates are sidecar admission and cold unit compilation, subject to narrower profiles before any new cache or rewrite. Compact bytecode, a full tracing heap, and native compilation remain deferred until current remaining-cost evidence meets their gates.
